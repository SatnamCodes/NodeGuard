Why a graph, not a flat feature table

A single mule account often doesn't look suspicious on its own: normal-ish transaction count, normal-ish amounts. What gives it away is who it's connected to. A chain of accounts passing the same money along fast, or a cluster of ordinary accounts quietly feeding into one collection point. A flat per-account model can't see that, because "my neighbor also looks weird" isn't a column in a feature table. A GNN can. It passes information along edges, so a node's prediction is built from its own features and its neighbors'.
